# Sharp negative type of finite Ptolemaic metrics

**Evidence Press · Research mathematics · Version 1.0 · Unrefereed**

For a finite metric space, negative type determines when a power of its distances can be represented as squared Euclidean distances. This paper determines the sharp exponent available uniformly over Ptolemaic metric spaces of a fixed cardinality. For every integer \(m\ge6\), it proves

\[
P(m)=\log_2\!\left(1+\frac{m}{\lfloor(m-1)^2/4\rfloor}\right),
\]

with strict negative type below the endpoint. Complete split metrics give matching upper bounds. Together with cited smaller cases, the formula resolves the rank-two uniform threshold conjecture of Baker, Huh, Kummer and Lorscheid. The first newly determined value relative to the cited predecessor is \(P(7)=\log_2(16/9)\).

The proof combines a dimension-independent packing inequality for powered stars with a recursive bound on normalised inverse entries. A separate cardinality induction supplies strictness only for smaller metrics, and continuity handles possibly singular endpoints.

**Assurance:** the universal claim rests on an analytic proof. The evidence includes exact and symbolic checks, preserved review and audit artefacts, and bounded numerical corroboration with explicit missingness and optimiser diagnostics. No proof-assistant verification or externally authenticated specialist peer review is claimed. The result does not classify all extremising metrics or determine every matroid threshold.

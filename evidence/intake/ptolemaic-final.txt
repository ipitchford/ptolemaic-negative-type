1.1   Relation to earlier work
Baker et al. (2026, Conjecture 6.4 and Proposition 7.5) formulated (1.5) in the study of Lorentzian
polynomials and weak representations over triangular hyperfields. They identified 𝑞(𝑛) with 𝑃(𝑛 − 1)
and proved the cases 𝑃(3) and 𝑃(4). Ercan (2026, Theorem 1.1) proved 𝑃(5) = log2 (9/4) and
𝑃(6) = 1, using partial correlations, a computer-assisted four-point estimate and coefficient transport
under metric inversion. Our proof independently recovers 𝑃(6); the newly determined values begin at
𝑃(7) and include every larger cardinality. The external five-point endpoint enters only in corollary 1.2;
it is not used in the proof of theorem 1.1.
    Korea Superintelligence Labs (2026, Version 3, Sections 11–12) identified the general anchor
constants and their complete-split thresholds, and established an obstruction above exponent one.
That preprint leaves the general low-exponent estimate unresolved (Section 15). Accordingly, the
constants and split thresholds themselves are not claimed as new here. The contribution is the universal
star-packing estimate and inverse-correlation induction below one, with their strictness hypotheses
discharged by the cardinality induction.
    The matrix language is classical. Schoenberg’s theory identifies negative type with Euclidean
embeddability of the corresponding snowflake (Schoenberg, 1938). Strict negative type and its exponent
range were studied by Li and Weston (2010); inverse-distance criteria for the supremal exponent appear
in Sánchez (2012). Augmented distance matrices and their relation to circumcentres belong to distance
geometry (Fiedler, 2011). We reproduce the identities needed here. The contribution of the present
argument is the star-packing estimate in theorem 3.4 and its use in the inverse-correlation induction
of theorem 5.1. These replace the need for separate higher-cardinality sign-pattern analyses: packing
handles the nonnegative inverse-vector case in every dimension, and a recursive inverse-entry bound
handles the remaining signs. Inversion, Schur complements and the complete split witnesses retain the
attributions above.

1.2   Structure of the proof
For 0 < 𝑝 < 1, the exact star with prescribed distances from a root gives an entrywise lower bound for
the Schoenberg matrix of any metric with those root distances. Direct monotonicity of the relevant

inverse pairing fails. Instead, a packing theorem controls a nonnegative subsolution of the star’s linear
system. This proves the required inverse bound whenever either of two inverse vectors is nonnegative.
   For the other sign patterns, we select a most negative normalised inverse entry. Deleting a third index
either makes it more negative, creates a controlled negative triangle, or forces the nonnegative-vector
case. The dimension-dependent constants satisfy the recursion required by this deletion argument.
   Finally, a coefficient vector on an 𝑚-point metric has a smaller sign class of size 𝑎 ≤ 𝑚/2. Every
pairwise Schur estimate then involves only 𝑎 + 2 ≤ 𝑚 − 1 points. The strict-negative-type induction
therefore supplies its hypotheses at the same exponent from strictly smaller metrics. We prove the
endpoint by pointwise continuity after the strict induction has closed.

8     Scope, equality and reproducibility
The proof determines a universal exponent and supplies a family attaining it. It does not classify every
metric attaining that exponent. Nor does it give a metric-independent positive spectral gap below the
endpoint: rescaling the original distances alone changes Gram eigenvalues without changing negative
type.
   The analytic argument uses no numerical minimisation, bounded-ratio reduction or finite covering of
metric space. The packing step optimises over a bounded polytope for each fixed length vector. The
inverse step selects a largest magnitude from finitely many entries of one fixed inverse matrix. Endpoint
passage is pointwise in the metric and coefficient vector.
